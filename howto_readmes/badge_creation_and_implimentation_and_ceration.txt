BADGES: CREATION, IMPLEMENTATION AND WHERE EVERYTHING LIVES
============================================================

Badges are 3D medals players earn by playing (Blackout Relay, Last Light) and
by opening crates. Each badge watches one STAT and has a ladder of
THRESHOLDS. Crossing the first threshold awards the badge, and every later
threshold LEVELS IT UP, the way a Strange counter climbs its ranks. Each level
is a new metal plus more ornament, so the badge looks more impressive the
further it climbs:

    Level 1  Bronze    the plate, its enamel face and the emblem
    Level 2  Silver    a raised, riveted rim
    Level 3  Gold      a laurel wreath round the bottom
    Level 4  Platinum  ribbon tails and a gem set at the top
    Level 5  Diamond   wings, and gems round the rim
    Level 6  Mythic    an obsidian plate, a crown, a ring of glowing beads,
                       and sparkles in its pop-up

A badge can have fewer than six levels (Sudden Death has 3, Grand Tour has 4).
It cannot have more: there are six tiers.

Where players see badges:
  * PROFILE:        a "Badges" panel shows four of them. Hovering one opens a
                    pop-up with the badge turning on a stand (drag it round),
                    its one-sentence description, its rank, the level ladder
                    and progress to the next rank.
  * PROFILE SETTINGS (/profile-editor), top of the page: the BADGE
                    INVENTORY. Every badge there is: earned ones in their
                    metal, the rest locked with a progress bar. Four slots
                    choose which ones the profile shows (drag in or click,
                    drag out or x to remove, drop on another to swap). With
                    no slots chosen, the profile shows the player's best four.
  * EDIT AVATAR (/avatar, "Badge" tab): pick one badge to WEAR. It is
                    pinned on the RIGHT of the CHEST (the character's right,
                    which is -X), small but readable, and it shows everywhere
                    the avatar does: profile, thumbnails, and in game.
  * NOTIFICATIONS:  the first award and every level-up leave a notification.
                    On the site it is a medal toast in the corner. In game,
                    the host shows a medal drop-in at the top of the screen
                    and a line in the chat.

2. HOW A BADGE GETS EARNED (the flow)
------------------------------------

  world code                       self.badge(player, "br_captures")
     |                                 (GameInstance.badge -> host.report_badge)
  game host                        adds it to a tally keyed (user, stat, mode)
     |  every heartbeat (~2.5 s)   one "badges" report per player:
     |                             {"kind": "badges", "user_id": 42,
     |                              "stats": [["br_captures", 1, "add"], ...]}
  web server /internal/heartbeat   badges.record_many(42, stats)
     |                                - updates badge_stats in one transaction
     |                                - finds every badge watching those stats
     |                                - awards / levels up, writes badge_awards
     |                                - pushes a notification (not for bots)
     |  reply {"badges": [level-up events]}
  game host                        announce_badges: {"t": "badge", ...} to the
                                   player (HUD toast) + a chat line for all

Things that happen on the web server (crates) call badges.record_many
directly. Anything else can too: badges.record(user_id, "stat", 1).

MODES:
  "add"  a running count. The value is the total so far (kills, revives).
  "max"  a best-ever. The value only goes up (best wave cleared).

DERIVED STATS: a stat worked out from other stats. DERIVED in badges.py maps
"ll_areas" to the prefix "ll_area_". Whenever an "ll_area_<id>" stat changes,
ll_areas becomes the number of those stats above zero, which is how the Grand
Tour counts distinct areas. Use this for any "do X in N different places"
badge.


3. MAKING A NEW BADGE: STEP BY STEP
-----------------------------------

The example adds "Flag Saver" to Blackout Relay: pick up your own team's
dropped flag while an enemy is within 20 studs.

STEP 1 -- Decide the stat, the ladder and the words.
  * Stat id: short, prefixed by game: br_ (Blackout Relay), ll_ (Last Light),
    ev_ (an event), or none for site-wide (crates_opened).
  * Thresholds: about one session for level 1, a dedicated week for gold, a
    long time for mythic. Look at the existing ladders for scale (ll_kills
    runs 100 -> 40,000; br_captures runs 1 -> 300). Six rising numbers.
  * Ranks: one title per level, getting grander ("Courier" ... "Blackout
    Phantom"). They show on the pop-up, the toast and the chat line.
  * Description: ONE sentence. It is the hover text and should say what you
    do to earn it.
  * Unit: what the number counts ("flags saved"), used in progress text.

STEP 2 -- Add the entry to BADGES_LIST in app/models/badges.py.

    _badge("br_flag_saver", "Flag Saver", "blackout_relay", "br_saves",
           [1, 10, 30, 75, 150, 300],
           ["Lifeguard", "Rescuer", "Saviour", "Guardian", "Shepherd",
            "The Last Line"],
           "Returned your team's flag with an enemy close enough to take it.",
           "em_lifebuoy", "cog", "flags saved"),

  Arguments, in order: id, name, game ("blackout_relay" | "last_light" |
  "market" | "event"), stat, thresholds, ranks (one per threshold), the
  one-sentence description, emblem, family, unit. Keywords:
  mode="max" for best-ever stats, and event="<event id>" for event badges.

  FAMILY is the backplate and colour scheme:
    "cog"     Blackout Relay (an industrial relay station): a cog, deep blue
              enamel, blue ribbons, steel wings
    "shield"  Last Light: a heater shield, ember enamel, orange ribbons,
              white feathered wings
    "star"    crates and the market: a star, purple enamel, purple ribbons
    "moon"    events: a moon disc with a crescent, near-black enamel, orange
              ribbons, BAT wings at Diamond
  A new game should get a new family. Add it to FAMILIES (face colour,
  ribbon colour, wing colour, wing mesh) and add a plate branch in _build().
  Any flat mesh from static/js/engine/shapes.js works as a plate: disc, star,
  shield, cog, heart, chevron, tri.

STEP 3 -- Paint the emblem (static/js/engine/textures.js).
  Emblems are 128x128 STICKERS: transparent around the picture, drawn with
  the helpers next to the others:
      inked(ctx, pathFn, fill, width)    a filled shape with a dark keyline
      stroked(ctx, pathFn, colour, w)    a keylined stroke
      poly([[x,y], ...])                 a path function from points
      circlePath(x, y, r)                a circle path function
  Keep the picture inside a circle of radius ~50 around (64, 64): the rim
  covers anything further out from Silver up. Use the cream (CREAM) and one
  or two strong colours. It has to read at the size of a badge on a chest.

      emblem('em_lifebuoy', function (ctx) {
        stroked(ctx, circlePath(64, 64, 34), '#ff5a3c', 22);
        stroked(ctx, circlePath(64, 64, 34), CREAM, 8);
      });

  emblem() registers it both as a decal (for the 3D badge) and for
  Textures.paintEmblem (the 2D medals in toasts).

STEP 4 -- Record the stat where the thing happens.
  In a world (any GameInstance subclass):

      def return_flag(self, flag, by=None):
          threatened = by is not None and any(
              p.team != by.team and p.alive and math.dist(p.pos, flag.pos) < 20
              for p in self.players.values())
          super().return_flag(flag, by)
          if threatened:
              self.badge(by, "br_saves")

  * Call self.badge(player, stat[, amount[, mode]]). Never call the host or
    the database from game code. self.badge works with any host, including
    test doubles that do not count badges.
  * Count ONCE per event, at the place the game already decides the event
    happened (after the capture is confirmed, not when the player is near).
  * Bots are players: they earn badges too, and that is intended.

  On the web server:
      from app.models import badges
      badges.record(user_id, "crates_opened")            # +1
      badges.record_many(user_id, [("a", 1, "add"), ("b", 9, "max")])

STEP 5 -- (Bots) give them the stat.
  New bots get badges from their made-up history in bots/factory.py
  _badges(). Add a line deriving the new stat from that history, so bot
  profiles show the badge at believable levels.

STEP 6 -- Test it.
  * python3 -c "from app.models import badges; print(badges.model('br_flag_saver', 6)[:2])"
    builds the model without errors.
  * Award it to yourself on a dev server:
      python3 -c "from app.models import badges; print(badges.record(1, 'br_saves', 40))"
    then look at /profile-editor (Badge Inventory), hover it, wear it from
    /avatar#badge, and check your profile.
  * Look at all six levels side by side. The scratch harness pattern used
    for the originals: render badges.model(id, level) for level 1..6 with the
    engine scripts and compare. Every level should read as a step up.
  * Play: earn it in game and check the drop-in toast and the chat line.
  * python3 tools/sitetests.py (badges section) and python3 tools/gametests.py.


4. THE MODEL: HOW _build() MAKES A BADGE
----------------------------------------

The model is about 1 unit tall, centred on the origin, facing +Z, built from
part()/place() exactly like the hats (app/models/modeling.py). Parts use the
renderer's keys directly: t, p, s, c, r, m (material: "metal", "glass",
"neon"), a (alpha; -1 = sticker), decal.

  plate         family mesh in the tier's body colour, metal
  face          a smaller disc/shield in the family's enamel colour
  emblem        a sticker box with the emblem decal at z ~0.1
  rim (L2+)     a torus turned to face +Z, trim colour, 8 rivets
  laurel (L3+)  6 leaves a side round the bottom
  ribbons (L4+) two ribbon tails, family ribbon colour; a glass gem on top
  wings (L5+)   wing (or bat) meshes behind the plate; 4 rim gems
  mythic (L6)   obsidian plate, crown of cones with neon tips, 18 neon beads

Models are cached per (badge, level). A Mythic is about 65 parts, which is
why the game join ticket carries only {id, level} (and every item only by id):
the ticket rides in the socket URL, and the host rebuilds the models.

On the chest (avatar.js), the model is scaled to 0.34 (male) or 0.30
(female), placed at the upper right of the chest, and stood just proud of
whichever torso segment is at that height (trunkAt), turning and leaning with
the shoulders. If a new family is much wider than the others (big wings),
check it on both builds, from the front and at three-quarters.

6. EVENT BADGES
---------------

An event badge has game "event", family "moon" (or a new one), and
event="<event id>". It is only earnable while the event runs: book its stat
behind crates.event_active("<event id>"), as open_crate does for Hallowed
Harvest 2026 (stat ev_harvest_2026). In the Badge Inventory an UNEARNED event
badge only appears while its event is live, which is the advert for it.
Earned ones stay forever. That is the point.

Name event badges with the year ("Hallowed Harvest 2026") so next year's
event can have its own.


7. RULES OF THUMB
-----------------

  * One sentence for the description. No numbers in it: the ladder and the
    progress bar show those.
  * Level 1 should come in the first session or two of doing the thing.
    Mythic should be rare and visible: that is what makes the top level
    worth showing off.
  * Don't count something a player can farm alone in a corner (killing a
    teammate, suiciding, standing in the lobby). Count the thing at the
    moment the game has already decided it happened.
  * Never call the database from a game tick. Use self.badge; the host
    batches it into the heartbeat.
  * Badge ids, stat ids and emblem names are permanent. Players' awards are
    stored by badge id. Rename the display name freely, never the id.
  * Removing a badge from BADGES_LIST hides it everywhere (earned() filters
    unknown ids), but the rows stay, so putting it back restores everyone's.
